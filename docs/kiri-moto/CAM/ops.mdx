# Operations

Ops—not to be confused with [opps](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/opp-opps)—are the operations
applied to a model to remove material. The Operations tab also contains meta-operations
that represent parts of the manufacturing process but do not necessarily generate toolpaths.

Each operation has parameters which can be hovered over to reveal their description.  
![](/img/CAM/paramDetails.png)

When an operation's settings are changed, they update the defaults for that operation
when it is created in the future.

These operations include:

- Toolpath Operations
  - Global
    - [Outline](#outline)
    - [Level](#level)
    - [Rough](#rough)
    - [Contour](#contour)
    - [Register](#register)
  - Specific
    - [Drill](#drill)
    - [Trace](#trace)
    - [Pocket](#pocket)
- [Gcode](#gcode)
- Laser Operations
- Indexed Operations

## Global Operations

Operations that apply to all objects in the workspace.

### Outline

<ImageCarousel base="/img/CAM/example/outline/" images="outline" />

The Outline op generates a toolpath surrounding the edges of a part. It is highly configurable and can select both internal or external edges specifically. The Outline operation is great for:

- Cutting out many objects at once
- Generating a rough/finishing pass on an object
- Easily avoiding specific features like pockets or holes

### Level

<ImageCarousel base="/img/CAM/example/level/" images="level" />

The Level op creates a flat-surface clearing toolpath across a selected area. It is designed to remove material to a consistent depth, making it useful for preparing stock, flattening uneven surfaces, or cleaning up residual material. The Level operation is great for:

- Surfacing raw stock before detail cuts
- Creating uniform faces on complex objects
- Clearing large areas with consistent depth quickly

### Rough

<ImageCarousel base="/img/CAM/example/rough/" images="rough" />

The Rough op removes large volumes of material quickly using a stepped-down toolpath. It is typically the first pass in a multi-step workflow, clearing space for finishing tools without concern for fine detail. The Rough operation is great for:

- Rapid bulk material removal
- Preparing many parts for finishing simultaneously
- Clearing voids or flat faces for many objects at once
- Preparing a part for finish machining
- Preserving tool life by reducing cutting load in later passes

### Contour

<ImageCarousel base="/img/CAM/example/contour/" images="contour" />

The Contour op generates detailed toolpaths for complex organic surface geometries.  
It can trace along the X or Y axis and has configurable precision. It is useful for:

- Carving 3D shapes with organic or curved profiles
- Finishing complex wall geometries
- Cleaning up a part after [roughing](#rough)

Contour's **feeds & speeds** section contains spindle, cutting feed and entry
plunge rate. Plunge **0** (also used by older operations without this setting)
uses the global Z / plunge speed limit. A positive entry plunge is capped by
that global limit and the cutting feed.

For non-indexed, linear surface cuts, feed is limited by the actual Z component
in either direction: `Z speed = path feed × |Z change| / move length`.
Shallow descending curves can therefore use the cutting feed without exceeding
the global Z limit. Steep slopes are slowed as needed. Entries, repositioning,
pure-Z cuts, indexed moves and other operation types retain conservative handling.
Export rechecks the rounded/scaled coordinates and rounds affected feeds down.
The global Z limit and Contour cutting feed must be positive and finite.
Contour does not infer how much material a preceding Rough operation removed;
choose feeds for the actual cutter, material and remaining stock.

### Register

For X/Y holes, **Method** explicitly selects **Drill / peck** or **Helical mill**.
New operations default to Drill / peck (the last selected method may be remembered).
Existing operations without a method retain their previous diameter-based
behaviour and acquire the matching method when opened for editing.

Drill / peck shows plunge, peck depth, dwell and lift. It makes a tool-sized hole;
saved milling diameter, allowance, pitch and finish values remain stored but
inactive. Helical mill shows hole diameter, allowance, programmed diameter,
cutting/plunge feeds, pitch and finish circle. Its adjusted diameter must exceed
the cutter diameter and it requires a centre-cutting end mill. The programmed
diameter uses the same label size as other controls, with a bold value.


The Register panel and Flip information popup show advisory warnings when an enabled three-point
X/Y registration layout on either side conflicts with the intended flip axis.
Flip X normally matches three-point Register X; Flip Y matches Register Y.
The wrong axis exchanges the single-pin and paired-pin ends. Warnings repeat
when Flip is clicked but do not block flipping or alter settings/toolpaths.
Disabled operations and operations beyond the end-of-chain marker are ignored.
Two-point layouts and puzzle patterns are not automatically judged.

While the Flip panel is open, a gold line marks the rotation axis through the
stock and a purple arrow shows a 180° half-turn. Click the **ⓘ information**
button in the Flip panel to open the setup instructions, compatibility warnings
and guide legend.
The popup describes which machine-coordinate edges exchange: Flip X exchanges +Y/−Y, and Flip Y exchanges
+X/−X. These directions remain tied to the stock as the camera moves. Either
turning direction reaches the same final orientation. Closing the panel or
performing the flip removes the guide.


For X/Y registration, **hole diameter** is the nominal bore diameter.
**Diameter allowance** adds a non-negative amount to that diameter, with a
default of `0` (no adjustment). For example, `6 mm` plus `0.05 mm` allowance
programs a `6.05 mm` bore, increasing the cutter-centre radius by `0.025 mm`.
The panel shows **Programmed diameter** in the current workspace units.
Missing allowance values in existing operations mean zero. The adjusted
diameter determines cutter compensation and validation in Helical mill mode.
Drill / peck ignores the saved hole diameter and allowance; Helical mill
requires a positive nominal diameter larger than the cutter after allowance. Pin positions and spacing are unchanged. In Drill / peck mode, **step down / peck depth** controls
pecking (`0` is a continuous plunge). Use a drill or a centre-cutting,
plunge-capable end mill. Helical mill rejects an adjusted diameter no larger than the cutter.

Select Helical mill to cut an oversized hole with a centre-cutting end mill.
**Helix pitch** is the positive depth per revolution; **bottom finish circle**
adds a full revolution at final depth. Cutting feed and plunge feed apply;
peck, lift and dwell controls are hidden. Cutter compensation is automatic.
Concentric overlapping helices clear larger bores without leaving a core.
Pin positions, spacing and the puzzle-pattern modes retain their behaviour.

CAM export offers a **Single combined file**, **ZIP — one file per operation**,
and **Download selected operation**. Separate files use numbered, tool-aware
filenames, restart modal state, retract before initial XY positioning and
after cutting, and suppress M6 tool changes. They retain the selected device's
header/footer. Keep XY zero fixed and re-establish Z after a manual tool change;
later operations can depend on material removed by earlier ones.

The built-in **Genmitsu PROVerXL 6050 Plus** GRBL profile uses a 600 × 500 ×
115 mm work envelope and a spindle command scale of 1000, with no automatic
tool-change macro. Set stock spindle speed using the control-box knob.


<ImageCarousel base="/img/CAM/example/register/" images="register" />

The Register operation drills holes in the sides of a part,  
helping keep the part in the same place when it is flipped onto its opposite face.  
The operation has options to drill on different sides and can even generate a puzzle-piece-like pattern for registration.
For a three-point registration layout, **pin spacing** controls the center-to-center distance between
the paired holes. Wider spacing improves resistance to angular alignment error. A value of `0` keeps
the automatic spacing derived from the selected tool diameter.

## Specific Operations

Specific operations require selection of individual part features to apply operations to.  
These may overlap with Global operations but are generally more configurable.

### Drill

<ImageCarousel base="/img/CAM/example/drill/" images="drill" />

The Drill op creates pecking toolpaths for a drill tool to follow.  
It can also drill holes of other diameters and even mark holes instead of drilling.  
It is useful for:

- Generating a toolpath for a drill tool
- Selecting specific holes to remove material from
- Marking holes to be drilled manually

### Trace

<ImageCarousel base="/img/CAM/example/trace/" images="trace" />

The Trace operation is likely the most configurable, allowing for generation of toolpaths following loops or lines.  
It can trace on, inside, or outside a line and can take multiple passes to step down to the desired position.  
When the type parameter is set to "clear," it can even act like a [Pocket](#pocket),  
clearing the area above the shape created by the selected lines or loop.  
While the Trace op can do a lot, some specific use cases include:

- Engraving lettering or other text
- Drilling out a hole helically
- Outlining a part with a nonstandard offset
- Outlining only one part on the workspace

### Pocket

<ImageCarousel base="/img/CAM/example/pocket/" images="pocket" />

The Pocket operation takes a selection of polygon faces and generates a pocket toolpath that cuts down to them.  
The operation has options to expand and smooth the pocket selection, and the contour option even allows  
for an approximation of a v-bit carve. Some use cases specific to the Pocket op include:

- Creating one pocket in a part with multiple
- Attempting a v-carve
- Clearing a specific area of a part

### Gcode

<ImageCarousel base="/img/CAM/example/gcode/" images="gcode" />

The Gcode operation does not generate a toolpath but instead adds a line or lines of G-code to the output  
of the file. The Gcode operation is different from [Gcode Macros](/kiri-moto/gcode-macros),  
as it is not tied to any event  
but is output in the order it is included in the operations array.

## Laser Operations

Laser operations become available when "Enable Laser" is selected in the [Machine](./machines) tab.

These operations are specific to certain machines, like the Makera Carvera, and can be safely ignored for other machines.

## Indexed Operations

Indexed operations become available when "Indexed" is selected in the [Process Ops](./processOpts) tab.

These operations are specific to indexed stock and allow a user to create operations on different faces of a part.
